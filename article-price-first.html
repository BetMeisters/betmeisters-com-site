Price first: how we decide a tip is worth backing

We put a chance on every selection before we look at the odds. Here is the arithmetic, with a worked example.

BetMeisters deskWednesday 28 October 20264 min read

A good tip is not the team most likely to win. It is a selection whose price is bigger than it should be. To find those, we work out the chance first and compare it with the price second.

Step 1: turn the price into a chance

Any decimal price can be read as a chance: divide 1 by the price. A price of 2.00 (evens) says 50%. A price of 1.50 (1/2) says 67%.

Add up the chances for every outcome in a market and you get more than 100%. The extra is the bookmaker's margin.

Example: a football match

Home 2.10 → 47.6% · Draw 3.40 → 29.4% · Away 3.60 → 27.8%

Total 104.8%, so the margin is about 4.8%.

Step 2: write down our own chance

Before looking at the odds, we estimate the chance from form, team news, ratings and conditions. We write that number down and keep it, so it can be checked once the result is in.

Step 3: compare

Our fair price is 1 divided by our chance. If we rate the home side at 55%, our fair price is 1.82. The edge is our chance multiplied by the price, minus 1.

55% × 2.10 − 1 = +15.5% edge → a tip

55% × 1.80 − 1 = −1.0% edge → no bet

Number Cruncher Meister only tips with an edge of +5% or more. Small edges disappear quickly when prices move.

Step 4: stake by confidence

  • 0.5 points: a real edge with a named doubt, such as a late fitness test
  • 1 point: the standard tip
  • 1.5 to 2 points: a large edge backed by strong evidence. We never raise stakes to chase losses.

Step 5: check against the closing price

When an event starts, the market has absorbed all the news. If we keep getting better prices than the final ones, that is strong evidence of skill, even over a short run where luck still decides most results.
