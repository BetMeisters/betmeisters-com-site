The UK regulator is going after the money behind illegal betting sites: what it means for you

7.8 million web addresses referred, £40 million in suspected criminal funds flagged and 1,000+ cease and desist notices. The Gambling Commission is now targeting payments, adverts and suppliers, and its new survey shows who uses illegal sites.

Licensing and regulationEnforcement
By the BetMeisters desk17 November 20263 min read

Britain's Gambling Commission says its fight against illegal gambling websites is moving from the sites themselves to the money, assets and business models behind them. The regulator announced the change on 9 November, at the start of the first international Illegal Gambling Awareness Week, and backed it with a set of numbers that show how fast the problem, and the response, has grown.

What the Commission is doing

Taking down a website on its own does little: an illegal operator can reappear on a new address within hours. So the Commission says it will now focus on what keeps those operators running:

  • Payments: disrupting the ways illegal sites take money from customers.
  • Advertising: making it harder for them to reach British players.
  • Suppliers: cutting off access to the games and sportsbooks they rent from other companies.
  • Criminal funds: referring suspected proceeds to partner agencies so they can be frozen, and working with police to seize cash from illegal land-based gambling.

Sue Young, the Commission's Executive Director of Operations, put it bluntly: "If you run an illegal gambling operation, we are coming after you, your business and your money."

The numbers

In the 2026/27 financial year so far, the Commission says it has:

  • issued more than 1,000 cease and desist notices to illegal operators;
  • referred 7.8 million web addresses to search engines, and had 3 million removed from search results;
  • referred 2,200 websites for disruption action;
  • referred more than £40 million in suspected criminal funds to partner agencies since January.

The jump in the second quarter is striking: URL referrals rose from 147,721 in April to June to 7.7 million in July to September, an increase of more than 5,000%. That says more about the Commission's new tools than about a sudden explosion of sites, but it shows the scale of what is out there.

Who uses illegal sites: the new survey data

Four days earlier, on 5 November, the Commission published the first estimates from new questions in its Gambling Survey for Great Britain, designed to show whether people are likely to be using an illegal site, whether they realise it or not.

  • Across the whole adult population, 94.4% showed no sign of using an illegal gambling website.
  • 0.6% had used one in the past four weeks, and a further 0.4% in the past year.
  • Among people who gamble online, those figures rise to 2.5% in the past four weeks and a further 1.5% in the past year.
  • Use was higher among men than women, and in the past year most common among 18 to 24-year-olds.

Sue Young's warning was that "those consumers who use illegal operators are more likely to experience harm". The Commission will track the figures over time on an updated illegal gambling data dashboard.

Why it matters if you bet

An unlicensed site is not just a legal technicality. If it refuses to pay you, there is no regulator to complain to and no independent dispute service. It does not have to check your age, offer deposit limits or join GAMSTOP, and your money is not protected if it disappears. Illegal sites often market themselves on exactly the things UK rules restrict: huge bonuses, no checks and no limits.

The quickest protection is to check the licence before you sign up. Every site licensed to take bets from people in Great Britain is listed on the Gambling Commission's public register, with its account number and the exact web addresses it is allowed to use. Our step-by-step guide to checking a licence shows how, and every BetMeisters.news review starts with that check.

What happens next

The Commission says it will work more closely with police, technology companies and the financial sector on intelligence sharing and early warnings. With the money now the target, the next visible results are likely to be payment blocks and asset freezes rather than website take-downs. We will report them as they are published.

Need help? GambleAware's helpline is free and open 24 hours: 0808 8020 133 (England and Scotland) or 0808 2819 265 (Wales). Complaints about a gambling business go to the Gambling Commission through its contact page. 18+.
