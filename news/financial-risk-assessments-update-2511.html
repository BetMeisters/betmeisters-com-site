Affordability checks: when the new financial risk assessments start, and who they will apply to

The Gambling Commission says its formal decision is weeks away and the Stage 1 timetable comes at the end of the year. At first, checks apply only to net deposits over £5,000 in 24 hours; fewer than 3% of accounts will ever need one.

Licensing and regulationUKGeneral news
By the BetMeisters desk25 November 20264 min read

The new financial risk assessments for online betting and casino customers, often called affordability checks, are moving closer. In a blog post on 17 November 2026, the Gambling Commission's Director of Major Policy Projects and Evaluation, Helen Rhodes, set out where things stand and what happens next.

What a financial risk assessment is

The idea is that online operators use credit reference agency data to spot high-spending customers who may be in financial difficulty, without asking them for payslips or bank statements. The Commission says high-spending customers are two to four times more likely to have a debt management plan, and two to five times more likely to have had a default in the past year, than the wider population. It also says the checks should reduce the document requests that many customers dislike.

The thresholds

  • Stage 1 covers the largest operators and the highest spenders: net deposits over £5,000 in a rolling 24 hours for most customers aged 25 and over, and £2,500 for younger customers.
  • Fully implemented, the checks will apply to customers aged 25 and over with net deposits over £1,000 in 24 hours or £3,000 over 90 days, and for under-25s over £750 in 24 hours or £2,000 over 90 days, across all online operators.
  • The Commission says fewer than 3% of customer accounts will need an assessment once fully in place.

What happens next

  • Two implementation groups are working through the technical detail: one with operators and credit reference agencies, one focused on credit information.
  • The formal consultation response is due in the coming weeks.
  • The Stage 1 timetable will be confirmed at the end of the year.

Why it matters to bettors

For almost all bettors, nothing changes: the thresholds are net deposits, so they only bite on people depositing very large amounts. If you are a high-staking bettor, an assessment would use credit data rather than paperwork, which should mean fewer interruptions than today's document checks.
