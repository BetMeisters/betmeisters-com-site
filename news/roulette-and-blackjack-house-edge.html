Roulette and blackjack: the real house edge, the rules that change it and why no system beats it

Roulette and blackjack are the two classic table games, and they work very differently. Every roulette bet carries the same edge; blackjack's edge depends on the table rules and how you play. The numbers, bet by bet and rule by rule, and what they mean for how long your money lasts.

Our live casino guide gives the headline house edges. This guide goes deeper on the two most popular table games, because in both the rules on the table matter more than most players realise. Our RTP guide explains how a house edge turns into the 'return to player' figure casinos publish.

Roulette: one edge for (almost) every bet

A European wheel has 37 pockets: 1 to 36 and a single zero. Every bet pays as if there were only 36 pockets, and that gap is the house edge: 1 in 37, or 2.70%. An American wheel adds a double zero: 38 pockets, the same payouts, so the edge rises to 2 in 38, or 5.26%.

The edge is the same whichever bet you choose. Betting on red is not 'safer' in the long run than betting on 17: it wins more often and pays less, and both cost you the same share of your stake over time. The exception is the American five-number bet (0, 00, 1, 2 and 3), which pays 6 to 1 and carries an edge of about 7.89%, the worst bet on the table.

La partage and en prison

Some single-zero tables (often called French roulette) give back half your even-money stake when zero lands (la partage), or hold the bet for one more spin (en prison). On even-money bets that halves the edge to about 1.35%. If you play even-money bets, it is the best roulette table you can find.

Why roulette systems do not work

Every spin is independent, and every bet has a negative edge. No combination or sequence of negative-edge bets can add up to a positive one. Doubling after a loss (the Martingale) just turns many small wins into an occasional huge loss; a long enough losing run, which always comes in the end, wipes out every small win before it, and table limits stop the doubling anyway. Patterns, 'hot numbers' and 'due' colours do not exist on a fair wheel.

Blackjack: the edge depends on the rules and on you

Blackjack is different: your decisions change the outcome. Played with basic strategy, the mathematically best decision for every hand against every dealer card, a good six-deck game has a house edge of around 0.4% to 0.5%, among the lowest in the casino. Played on hunches, it is several times higher. And the table rules move the edge too:

RuleChange in house edge
Blackjack pays 6:5 instead of 3:2+1.39%
Dealer hits soft 17 (instead of standing)+0.22%

The baseline is a six-deck game where the dealer stands on soft 17, doubling after a split and late surrender are allowed, and the player uses perfect basic strategy (edge about 0.43%). The effects add up: a 6:5 table on its own more than triples the edge. Always check the blackjack payout printed on the table.

A full basic-strategy chart covers every hand, but a handful of decisions do most of the work. These are for a typical six-deck game where the dealer stands on soft 17:

The reasoning behind most of these is the dealer's chance of going bust. A dealer showing 4, 5 or 6 must draw to at least 17 and busts often, so you stand on weak totals and let the dealer take the risk. A dealer showing 7 or higher is likely to make a strong hand, so a 15 or 16 will usually lose if you stand.

Counting cards, continuous shufflers and online games

Card counting, keeping track of how many high cards are left, can tip the edge slightly towards a skilled player in a dealt game with deep penetration. It is not illegal, but casinos can refuse your play. Online and many live games reshuffle every hand or use continuous shuffling machines, which removes any benefit. For almost every player, the realistic goal is basic strategy on a good-rules table: the smallest edge the casino offers, not an edge for you.

Insurance and side bets

When the dealer shows an ace, you are offered insurance, a side bet paying 2 to 1 that the dealer has blackjack. For a basic-strategy player it is a poor bet, because the dealer has a ten-value card less than one time in three. Side bets such as 'perfect pairs' or '21+3' usually carry much higher edges than the main game.

What the edge means for your money

The house edge is a share of everything you stake, not of what you bring. At £10 a spin and about 60 spins an hour, you stake £600 an hour on roulette: at 2.70% that is an expected cost of about £16.22 an hour on a single-zero wheel and £31.58 on a double-zero one. At blackjack with basic strategy on a good table, £10 a hand for 70 hands is £700 staked, an expected cost of about £3.01. Live and online games play faster than you think, so the stake per hour adds up quickly.

Common questions

Which roulette has the best odds? Single-zero (European), and best of all single-zero with la partage or en prison on even-money bets.

Can you beat roulette? Not with any betting system. Every bet has a negative edge.

What is basic strategy in blackjack? A chart of the best decision for every combination of your hand and the dealer's up-card, worked out by computer. It does not beat the house, but it cuts the edge to well under 1% on a good table.

Is 6:5 blackjack worth playing? It adds about 1.4% to the edge, more than any other common rule. Find a 3:2 table.
