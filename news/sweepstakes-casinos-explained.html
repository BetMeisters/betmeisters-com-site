Sweepstakes casinos explained: how the two-coin model works, and the states banning it

Sweepstakes casinos sell one coin and give away another that can be swapped for cash prizes. How the model works, why US states increasingly call it gambling, which states have banned it, and why it is not a UK product.

Online casinoRegulation
By the BetMeisters desk27 November 20266 min read

A sweepstakes casino is a US website or app that offers slots, table games and sometimes sports picks without calling itself a casino. Instead of betting money directly, you play with virtual coins. The legal argument rests on how those coins work, and more and more US states have decided the argument does not hold.

How the two-coin model works

  • Coin one: you buy a pack of virtual coins to play for fun. They have no cash value.
  • Coin two: the pack comes bundled with a second, "free" bonus currency. Games played with this one can win cash or cash-equivalent prizes.
  • The pitch: because the prize coins are presented as a free giveaway rather than a stake, operators describe the games as free-to-play sweepstakes contests rather than gambling.

Critics, and now many state legislatures, see it differently: you pay money, you play a game of chance, and you can win money. That is the three-part test of gambling in most places. California's law, for example, treats the bonus currency as illegal gambling when it is bought directly or indirectly and can be swapped for cash or prize entries, even though it is labelled free.

Other states have acted through their regulators rather than new laws: Delaware, Idaho, Michigan and Washington have used cease-and-desist orders or existing bans. Louisiana's first attempt was vetoed in 2025 because the governor said the activity was already illegal; a new law followed in 2026.

It is not just the operators

The newer laws reach further than the websites themselves. California's AB 831 and several 2026 laws extend liability to suppliers, payment firms, geolocation providers, promoters and media affiliates. Penalties are serious: Indiana and Louisiana allow fines of up to $100,000, Louisiana up to five years in prison, and Oklahoma makes it a felony. Is there a UK version?

No. Sweepstakes casinos are a US model built around US state law. In Great Britain, playing a game of chance for a prize is gaming under the Gambling Act 2005, and anyone offering it to people in Britain needs a Gambling Commission licence. Sweepstakes casinos are not licensed in Britain as casinos, so they are not an option for UK players. If you want to play casino games in the UK, use a site that holds a Gambling Commission licence, which you can check on the public register.

If you are in the US: what to check

  • Your state's law first. In a growing number of states these sites are now illegal, and the newer laws can hit anyone who promotes them.
  • Prize redemption rules. Cashing out prize coins usually needs identity checks, minimum amounts and play-through rules. Read them before you buy anything.
  • Protections. A sweepstakes casino is not a state-licensed casino, so you do not get the oversight a state regulator gives licensed casino players.
  • The money is real. Whatever the coins are called, you are paying real money. Set limits and stop if it stops being fun.

FAQ

Are sweepstakes casinos legal? It depends on the state, and the list where they are banned is growing every year.

Why do they call it free to play? Because the legal model depends on the prize coins being a free giveaway rather than a stake. States that have banned them say that in practice players are paying to play for cash.

Can I use one from the UK? No. They are a US product and are not licensed in Britain.
