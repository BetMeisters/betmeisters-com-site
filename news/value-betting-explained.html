Value betting explained: chance, price and edge

The idea behind every BetMeisters tip, in three numbers you can check yourself.

FootballSports betting
By the BetMeisters desk16 November 20265 min read

Most betting advice asks who will win? Value betting asks a different question: is the price bigger than the chance deserves?

1. The price says what the bookmaker thinks

Decimal odds of 2.00 imply a 50% chance (1 ÷ 2.00). Odds of 4.00 imply 25%. This is the implied chance. Add up the implied chances for every outcome in a market and you get more than 100%: the extra is the bookmaker's margin.

2. The chance: your best estimate of the truth

BetMeisters' Number Cruncher uses the sharpest market in the world, Pinnacle, and removes its margin to get a fair chance. Example: if Pinnacle's prices make a team 55% to win, the fair price is 1 ÷ 0.55 = 1.82.

3. The edge: chance × price − 1

If a UK bookmaker offers 2.00 on that 55% team, the edge is 0.55 × 2.00 − 1 = +10%. On average, over many bets like it, you would expect to make about 10p for every £1 staked. At 1.70 the edge is 0.55 × 1.70 − 1 = −6.5%: the same team, a bad bet.

Why value bets still lose

A +10% edge on a 55% chance still loses 45% of the time. Edge only shows over hundreds of bets, which is why BetMeisters publishes every tip and every result, win or lose, rather than a highlights reel.

Three habits that help

  • Compare prices: the same bet is often 5–10% bigger at one bookmaker than another.
  • Use level stakes, so one bad week does not wipe you out.
  • Keep records. If you do not know your own results, you cannot tell luck from skill.

Even a real edge is no guarantee. Only bet what you can afford to lose.
