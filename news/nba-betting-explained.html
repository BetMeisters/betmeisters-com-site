NBA betting explained: point spreads, money lines, totals and the NBA Cup

The three main NBA markets, how overtime counts, why lines move on injury news, and what the NBA Cup adds in November and December. Written for UK and US readers, with US Eastern and UK times.

BasketballSports betting
By the BetMeisters desk24 November 20266 min read

The NBA regular season runs from late October to April, with games most nights. For bettors that means a lot of games and a lot of information: injuries, rest days and back-to-backs can all move a line within minutes. This guide covers the main markets and the things that matter most when you price them.

The game in numbers

An NBA game is four 12-minute quarters, 48 minutes in all. If the scores are level after 48 minutes there is a five-minute overtime, repeated until there is a winner. A game cannot end in a draw. Most games tip off between 7pm and 10.30pm US Eastern time, which is midnight to 3.30am in the UK.

1. The money line

The money line is a straight bet on which team wins, overtime included. Favourites are short and underdogs are long. US sites show the price in American odds: −200 means you stake $200 to win $100 (2.50 in decimal is +150, 1.50 is −200). Our tips show decimal odds, and you can switch to fractional or US on the site.

2. The point spread (handicap)

The spread is the most popular NBA market. The favourite gives up points, the underdog gets them. If the Celtics are −7.5 against the Hornets, the Celtics need to win by 8 or more for the bet to win. A Hornets +7.5 bet wins if the Hornets win, or lose by 7 or fewer. Prices on both sides are usually close to evens (about 1.91, or −110).

Spreads with a whole number, such as −7, can land exactly on the line. That is a push, and your stake is returned. The half point (−7.5) removes the push.

3. Totals (over/under)

A total is a bet on the combined points scored by both teams, overtime included. NBA totals are usually somewhere between about 210 and 240 points, depending on the teams' pace. An overtime period adds roughly 20 points to the total, so it can turn a close Under into a loser in the last minute.

Overtime and settlement

Most bookmakers settle NBA money lines, spreads and totals including overtime, and quarter or half bets only on that period. Some UK bookmakers also offer a three-way "match result" market settled at the end of regulation time, where a draw after 48 minutes is a possible result. The rules differ by bookmaker, so check them before you bet.

Player props and other markets

Player markets, such as a player's points, rebounds or assists, are popular but carry bigger margins and are hit hardest by late news: a player who is ruled out or has his minutes cut can make the line meaningless. Some bookmakers void a prop if the player does not play. Others may not.

What moves an NBA line

  • Injury reports. Teams must report player availability before games, and the status of a star can move a spread by several points. Late scratches are common, so lines can move sharply in the hour before tip-off.
  • Rest and back-to-backs. A team playing its second game in two nights, especially after travel, often rests players or tires late.
  • Home court. Home advantage in the NBA is real but smaller than many people think, worth a couple of points in most models.
  • Pace. Fast teams push totals up. Two slow, defensive teams push them down.

The NBA Cup

The Emirates NBA Cup runs inside the regular season. In 2026 the group games are played on set "Cup Nights": 30 October and 6, 13, 20, 24, 25 and 27 November. The quarter-finals follow on 4 and 5 December, the semi-finals on 8 and/or 9 December, and the championship game is on 11 December at Hinkle Fieldhouse in Indianapolis. Group games also count in the normal standings, so they are priced like any other game. In group play, though, point differential can decide who goes through, which can change how a team plays late in a game it has already won.
