NFL betting explained: point spreads, money lines, totals and how overtime counts

The three main NFL markets, why 3 and 7 are the numbers that matter most, how regular-season overtime and ties work, and what to check before you bet. Written for UK and US readers.

American footballSports betting
By the BetMeisters desk25 November 20265 min read

The NFL season runs from September to February, with most games on Sundays plus Thursday and Monday nights, and a Thanksgiving triple-header in late November. There are fewer games than in the NBA or the Premier League, so every line gets a lot of attention, and the prices are among the sharpest in sport.

The three main markets

  • Point spread (handicap). The favourite gives points, the underdog gets them. Chiefs −3.5 must win by 4 or more; Bills +3.5 win the bet if they win, or lose by 3 or fewer. Spreads are usually priced near even money on both sides, around 1.91 (−110 in US odds).
  • Money line (match winner). Simply who wins. Favourites are short, underdogs are longer.
  • Total (over/under). The combined points of both teams, for example over or under 44.5.

Why 3 and 7 matter

Most NFL points come in threes and sevens: a field goal is worth 3, and a touchdown with the extra point is worth 7. So a large share of games end with one team winning by exactly 3 or exactly 7. That is why moving a spread from −2.5 to −3.5 changes the bet far more than moving it from −5.5 to −6.5, and why bookmakers charge more to buy a half point around 3 and 7.

Overtime and ties

If the scores are level after four quarters in the regular season, there is one 10-minute overtime period. Both teams are guaranteed a possession unless the first team scores a defensive touchdown or a safety, or the first drive uses the whole 10 minutes. If it is still level after 10 minutes, the game ends in a tie. In the play-offs there is no tie: overtime continues until someone wins.

  • Spreads and totals usually include overtime.
  • A tie affects the money line: many bookmakers settle a two-way money line as void on a tie, while a three-way market (home, tie, away) pays the tie. Check the rules before you bet.

What moves an NFL line

  • Quarterbacks. No single player moves a line like the starting quarterback. Injury reports come out during the week and the final inactive list about 90 minutes before kick-off.
  • Weather. Strong wind hurts passing and kicking and tends to push totals down.
  • Short weeks and travel. Thursday games and long trips can matter, though the market prices these in quickly.

Times for UK readers

Sunday's early games start at 1pm US Eastern, which is 6pm UK time, with late games about 9.05pm or 9.25pm UK and the Sunday night game at 1.20am UK on Monday. Our Thanksgiving preview lists this week's games with UK and US times.
