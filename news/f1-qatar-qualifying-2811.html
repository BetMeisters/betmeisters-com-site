Qatar Grand Prix qualifying preview: every team-mate battle, priced by the sharp market

Not a tip: a preview. Saturday night under the Lusail floodlights, the sharp market's chance for all 11 team-mate head-to-heads in qualifying, set against what Friday practice showed.

This is a preview, not a tip. BetMeisters only tips when a UK price beats the fair price from the sharp market, confirmed by two bookmakers. For qualifying head-to-heads we have the sharp price but no UK bookmaker prices in our data, so there is nothing to tip. What we can do is show what the sharpest market thinks of every team-mate battle tonight, and where Friday's practice times tell a different story.

When it happens

  • Final practice: Saturday 2.30pm UK (5.30pm in Qatar).
  • Qualifying: Saturday 6pm UK (9pm in Qatar), under floodlights.
  • Grand Prix: Sunday 4pm UK (7pm in Qatar), 57 laps of the 5.419km Lusail circuit.

It is round 22, the penultimate race of the season, and a standard weekend: three practice sessions and no Sprint. The main straight is over a kilometre long, the biggest chance to pass into Turn 1, so grid position matters here.

Where the season stands

The titles are settled. Kimi Antonelli clinched the drivers' championship in Mexico on 1 November, and Mercedes took the constructors' title in Sao Paulo a week later. Max Verstappen arrives having won in Las Vegas. What is left to race for is wins, places in the standings and pride between team-mates, which is exactly what qualifying head-to-heads measure.

What Friday told us

Antonelli topped both practice sessions, 0.507s clear of Russell in the first and 0.127s clear in the second (1:23.077). McLaren were next, Norris and Piastri split by 0.027s, with the Red Bulls of Verstappen and Hadjar 0.007s apart just behind. Ferrari were a second off: Leclerc spun through the gravel and Hamilton's car stopped late on with a suspected power-unit problem. Two red flags extended the session by 13 minutes, and the Cadillacs of Bottas and Perez were last.

Practice times are a rough guide only: teams run different fuel loads and programmes, and nobody shows everything on a Friday. That is why the sharp market does not simply copy the timesheet.

Team by team

  • Antonelli v Russell (Mercedes): Antonelli 70%. Antonelli topped both Friday sessions; Russell was 0.127s behind him in FP2.
  • Verstappen v Hadjar (Red Bull): Verstappen 74%. Only 0.007s apart in FP2 (5th and 6th), yet the market makes Verstappen a clear favourite.
  • Norris v Piastri (McLaren): Norris 61%. 0.027s apart in FP2. Piastri took pole here a year ago, 0.108s ahead of Norris.
  • Leclerc v Hamilton (Ferrari): Leclerc 62%. Leclerc spun into the gravel in FP2; Hamilton's car stopped late on with a suspected power-unit problem.
  • Lawson v Lindblad (Racing Bulls): Lawson 50%. A true coin flip: Lawson was 0.122s quicker in FP2 after 14th in FP1.
  • Bortoleto v Hulkenberg (Audi): Bortoleto 60%. Bortoleto was 0.257s quicker in FP2 (11th v 13th).
  • Gasly v Colapinto (Alpine): Gasly 74%. Gasly was 0.969s quicker in FP2, despite stopping in the gravel and causing a red flag.
  • Sainz v Albon (Williams): Sainz 50%. Priced as a coin flip, but Albon was 0.563s quicker in FP2.
  • Bearman v Camara (Haas): Bearman 71%. Camara was 0.034s quicker in FP2, yet the market makes Bearman a 71% favourite.
  • Alonso v Stroll (Aston Martin): Alonso 85%. Alonso is back after illness kept him out of Thursday's media day; he was 0.602s up on Stroll in FP2.
  • Perez v Bottas (Cadillac): Perez 65%. Last two in FP2, 0.117s apart. Perez also carries a five-place grid penalty from Las Vegas.

Three where practice and price disagree

Verstappen v Hadjar. Seven thousandths of a second apart on Friday, but the market still makes Verstappen a 74% favourite. It is pricing in his record over a single lap, not one practice session.

Bearman v Camara. Camara, who gave up his Formula 2 title fight to step up with Haas, was quicker in FP2, yet Bearman is a 71% favourite. The market is backing the more experienced driver over one practice lap.

Sainz v Albon. A 50-50 price, even though Albon was over half a second quicker on Friday. The market is treating that gap as noise.

None of this makes either driver a bet. A fair price only becomes value when someone offers more than it, and that is the test we apply before every BetMeisters tip.

How head-to-heads usually work

A qualifying head-to-head is won by the driver who qualifies higher. Markets usually settle on the official qualifying classification, before grid penalties, so Perez's five-place penalty would not normally change his head-to-head with Bottas. Rules differ, so check how your bookmaker settles a driver who does not set a time.
