Online casino is driving almost all of Britain's gambling growth, while betting shops keep closing

The Gambling Commission's policy director says total gambling yield grew 4.4% to £17.5 billion, but online casino accounted for nearly all of it. Betting shops fell 3.6% to 5,617 in a year.

Licensing and regulationOnline casinoUKGeneral news
By the BetMeisters desk24 November 20264 min read

Britain's gambling market looks stable on the surface, but almost all its growth now comes from one place: online casino. That was the message from the Gambling Commission's Director of Policy, Ian Angus, in a speech to the Institute of Licensing National Training Conference on 19 November 2026, published on the Commission's website.

The headline numbers

  • Gross gambling yield (what operators keep after paying out winnings) reached £17.5 billion in the 2025/26 financial year, up 4.4%.
  • Online casino yield rose by £736 million, more than the £728 million rise for all licensed gambling put together. In other words, the rest of the market shrank slightly overall.
  • Land-based gambling yield rose just 1.1%. Betting shops' yield fell, while adult gaming centres grew 11% and non-remote bingo 8%.
  • Past-four-week gambling participation was stable at 47% in 2025, but only 27% once people who only play lottery draws are left out.

Fewer betting shops

The number of gambling premises in Great Britain fell 2% to 8,081, and betting shops fell 3.6% to 5,617. Adult gaming centres went the other way, from 1,417 to 1,449. Angus said the industry is going through "a sustained period of change", including recent tax changes, which he said were a matter for the Government rather than the Commission.

What is changing next

  • Gambling Impact Assessments come into force this month, letting councils weigh local evidence such as crime, deprivation and the number of existing premises when assessing new gambling businesses.
  • The Government is expected to consult on the "aim to permit" principle in the Gambling Act 2005, which currently tells councils to aim to grant premises licences.
  • New rules for the highest-stake category B gaming machines will bring session information and better gambling-management tools, with most of the changes taking effect from June 2027. Existing machines are exempt.
  • Council gambling inspections rose 29% in 2025/26, and the Commission has drawn down the first £7 million of £26 million in Government funding over three years to tackle illegal gambling, including raids on illegal gambling dens.

Why it matters to bettors

For sports bettors, fewer shops means more betting moves online, where limit tools, affordability checks and the new rules apply. The growth figures also show where the industry's money is coming from: online casino, not sports betting.
