Paddy Power, Betfair and William Hill: what the Gambling Commission's register shows

A £2 million settlement for the Betfair and Paddy Power licences, and a self-reported fine for William Hill. Here is what each action was for, and how it feeds into our reviews.

EnforcementLicensing and regulationGeneral news
By the BetMeisters desk16 November 20266 min read

The UK Gambling Commission publishes every fine, settlement and warning on its public register. We check that register for every operator we review, because a bookmaker's regulatory record says more about how it treats customers than its advertising does. Here are the most recent actions for three of the biggest UK brands, and what they mean.

Paddy Power and Betfair: £2 million settlement (17 December 2025)

The licence behind paddypower.com and betfair.com is held by PPB Entertainment Limited (account 39426). The register records a settlement dated 17 December 2025, made up of a payment in lieu of a financial penalty, a public statement and the Commission's costs.

The total was £2 million, shared across four licensees: PPB Entertainment Limited, PPB Counterparty Services Limited, Betfair Casino Limited and TSE Malta LP. The register does not split the figure between them.

The failings were in customer interaction, the rules that require operators to spot customers at risk of harm and act on it. The register cites breaches of paragraphs 1, 4, 7, 8 and 11 of Social Responsibility Code Provision 3.4.3. The action followed a 2024 compliance assessment of the Betfair and Paddy Power remote licences.

William Hill: £82,687 fine (7 August 2025)

William Hill's online site, williamhill.com, runs under WHG (International) Limited (account 39225), now part of evoke plc. The register shows one action: a financial penalty of £82,687 dated 7 August 2025.

It was for a failure on identifying individual remote customers, a breach of paragraph 3 of Social Responsibility Code Provision 3.9.1. The register notes that the company self-reported the breach and co-operated with the investigation. That matters: a self-reported, relatively small fine is a very different signal from a large settlement uncovered by the regulator.

Ladbrokes and Coral: no actions on the register now

The online licence for ladbrokes.com and coral.co.uk is held by LC International Limited (account 54743), part of Entain. Its register page currently shows no regulatory actions.

That does not mean a spotless history. In August 2022 the Commission announced that Entain would pay £17 million for social responsibility and anti-money-laundering failures, of which £14 million related to LC International. It was the largest penalty of its kind at the time. Under our scoring method, the regulatory record looks back three years, so the 2022 settlement falls outside the scored window. We still mention it in the review, because readers deserve the full picture.

How this goes into our reviews

Every BetMeisters.news review scores regulatory record as part of the Licence module, on a simple scale: no actions in three years scores 10, one minor fine that has been fixed scores 5, and a major sanction or suspension scores 0.

We link straight to the register entry in every review, so you can check it yourself. That is the point: you should not have to take our word for it, or the bookmaker's.

How to check any operator yourself

Search the operator's company name or account number on the Gambling Commission's public register. The Regulatory actions tab lists fines and settlements, and the Domain names tab shows which websites the licence covers. Our step-by-step guide to checking a licence walks through it.
