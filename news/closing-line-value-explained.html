Closing line value explained: why beating the final price matters more than one result

The closing line is the last price before an event starts, when the market has seen the most money and the most information. Taking a better price than that, again and again, is the clearest early sign that a bettor has a real edge. Here is how it works and how we measure it.

Sports betting
By the BetMeisters desk25 November 20265 min read

Every price moves between the moment it is first offered and the moment the event starts. The last price before the start is the closing line. Closing line value (CLV) compares the price you took with that closing price. If you backed a team at 2.20 and it started at 2.00, you beat the close. If it drifted to 2.40, you did not.

Why the closing price is the benchmark

By the start, the market has absorbed nearly everything: team news, line-ups, weather, and the bets of the best-informed customers at the sharpest bookmakers and exchanges. That makes the closing price, with the bookmaker margin removed, the best single estimate of the real chance of each outcome. Beating it consistently over a large number of bets suggests you are finding value that the market later agrees with.

Why it matters more than results

A single bet tells you almost nothing. A good price can lose and a bad price can win. Even a hundred results can mislead, because luck swings a long way over short runs. CLV is different: it is known the moment the event starts, it does not depend on who wins, and it settles down far faster than profit does. That is why serious bettors and bookmakers both watch it.

How to work it out

  • Simple version: your price divided by the closing price, minus 1. Back at 2.20, close at 2.00: 2.20 ÷ 2.00 − 1 = +10%.
  • Better version: remove the bookmaker's margin from the closing prices first, to get the fair chance of each outcome, then compare your price with the fair price. A closing price of 2.00 with a margin built in may mean a fair price nearer 2.08.
  • Use a sharp closing price. A soft bookmaker's last price is not a good benchmark. Exchanges and low-margin bookmakers are.

What it does not tell you

  • CLV is not profit. You can beat the close and still lose over a short run.
  • It needs volume. A handful of bets proves little either way.
  • Thin markets can mislead: a closing price in a small league with little money behind it is a weaker benchmark than a Premier League or NBA close.
