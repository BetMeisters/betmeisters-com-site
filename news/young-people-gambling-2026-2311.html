Fewer teenagers are gambling with their own money, but 9 in 10 saw gambling adverts

The Gambling Commission's Young People and Gambling 2026 survey of 3,867 pupils aged 11 to 17 found 26% spent their own money on gambling in the past year. Arcade machines lead, and 22% opened loot boxes.

Safer gamblingLicensing and regulationUK
By the BetMeisters desk23 November 20264 min read

A quarter of 11 to 17 year-olds in Great Britain spent their own money on gambling in the past year, according to the Gambling Commission's Young People and Gambling 2026 report, published on 12 November 2026 as official statistics.

The survey, run in schools by Ipsos, covered 3,867 pupils aged 11 to 17 in England, Scotland and Wales, who answered online questionnaires in class between January and June 2026.

The headline numbers

  • 26% spent their own money on any gambling activity in the 12 months before the survey. A reported comparison with 2022 puts that figure at 31%, though the 2022 sample did not include Year 12 pupils or independent schools, so the change is indicative rather than exact.
  • Most of that was not age-restricted: arcade machines such as penny pushers and claw grabbers were the most common activity, at about 19%, and around 11% had bet with friends or family.
  • About 5% had taken part in age-restricted gambling, the kind the law reserves for adults.
  • 7% gambled online in the past year, including 3% on a betting site or app and 3% on an online casino game.
  • 0.9% scored as problem gamblers on the youth version of the problem gambling screen, which reports say has been roughly stable since 2022, with 2.0% classed as at risk.

Adverts and online content

Almost 9 in 10 young people (89%) recalled seeing or hearing gambling advertising or promotions in the past year. Weekly exposure was highest on social media (55%) and live streaming and video sites (53%), ahead of television. Reports of the findings say about 15% followed gambling-related content online, and that 38% of those said influencers or streamers had advertised gambling to them.

Loot boxes and parents' accounts

The report also tracks gambling-like features in video games: 22% had opened loot or surprise boxes with their own money. Among young people who gambled online, reports of the survey say 29% used a parent's or guardian's account with permission and 8% without it.

Why it matters

Most of the gambling in these figures is in activities without an age limit, such as arcade machines and private bets between friends, rather than bets with licensed bookmakers. The advertising figures show how widely gambling content reaches young people online. The next edition is due in November 2027.

If you are worried about a young person and gambling, free confidential help is available from the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133, and from GamCare.
