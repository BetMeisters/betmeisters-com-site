Tuesday night in the US is a full card: dozens of college basketball games, five NBA games and eleven NHL games. The headline game in college hoops is Duke v Illinois; in the NBA, the Knicks are in Toronto.

The chances below come from Pinnacle, the sharpest bookmaker in the market, with its margin removed, from BetMeisters' scan at 7pm UK (2pm ET). They show what the market thinks. US markets are mostly spreads and totals: where we quote a spread or total, it is the line the market prices closest to 50/50. Times are UK, with US Eastern in brackets.

College basketball

Duke v Illinois · midnight UK (7.00pm ET)

The marquee game of the night. The market makes Duke 77.5% to win and Illinois 22.5%. The spread sits at Duke −8, priced close to 50/50, and the total at about 167, the highest of the big college games tonight. A Duke win is the expected result; whether they win by more than eight is the real question.

Big spreads

Early-season college schedules include a lot of mismatches. The market's biggest lines tonight include Miami −41.5 against Rider, Vanderbilt −41.5 against Eastern Illinois, Xavier −33 against Cleveland State, DePaul −30.5 against Maryland Eastern Shore and Providence −30 against Sacred Heart. Spreads that big are close to 50/50 by design, and the end of the game matters: a big favourite that rests its starters can fail to cover a 40-point line even while winning easily.

Hockey money lines include overtime and shootouts unless the market says '60 minutes' or 'regulation time'.

Reading US lines

US markets look different from the football markets most UK readers know. Three things are worth knowing before a night like this.

  • Spreads are set to split the money, not to predict the margin exactly. When the market makes Duke −8 a 50/50 proposition, it is saying that Duke winning by nine or more is as likely as anything else. A favourite at 77.5% to win can still be a coin flip against the spread.
  • Half-points matter. A spread of −8 can push (stake returned) on an eight-point win; −8.5 cannot. In college basketball the most common winning margins sit in single figures, so moving across a key number changes the chance more than it looks.
  • Totals include overtime. In basketball, an overtime period adds points to the total, which is a small boost for overs in close games. Hockey totals and money lines include overtime and shootouts unless they say otherwise.

The money-line chances above are the clearest guide to who is likely to win. If you want a quick way to see how one-sided a game is, compare the two: Spurs at 79.2% with a 10-point spread, Lakers at 72.4% with 7 points, Knicks at 61.3% with 4.5.

Early-season form

A month into the NBA season, the market still leans partly on pre-season ratings, and each new result moves it. That is why spreads and totals can move a point or two through the day, especially when injury news lands. In college basketball, November games come before conference play, so many lines rest on a handful of games and last season's ratings. That is one reason college spreads move more than NBA spreads between the opening line and tip-off.
