Betting exchanges explained: backing, laying, commission and why the prices are sharper

On an exchange you bet against other people, not a bookmaker. You can back a selection to win or lay it to lose, and the exchange takes a commission on winnings instead of building a margin into the odds. Here is how it works, with worked examples.

Sports betting
By the BetMeisters desk24 November 20266 min read

A normal bookmaker sets the odds and takes the other side of your bet. A betting exchange works differently: it is a marketplace where customers bet against each other. One person offers odds, another accepts them, and the exchange matches the two and holds the money until the result is in.

Because the exchange does not need to win your bet to make money, it does not build a profit margin into the prices. It charges a commission on winnings instead. That is why exchange prices are often close to the true chance of an outcome, and why we use the sharpest markets as our benchmark for every BetMeisters tip.

Back and lay

  • Back a selection and you win if it happens, exactly like a normal bet. Back Arsenal at 2.00 with £10 and you win £10 profit if Arsenal win.
  • Lay a selection and you win if it does not happen. You are acting as the bookmaker. Lay Arsenal at 2.00 for £10 and you win the £10 stake from the backer if Arsenal draw or lose, but you pay out £10 if Arsenal win.

Liability: the number to watch when you lay

When you lay, what you can lose is your liability, and it grows with the odds. The sum is simple: liability = stake × (odds − 1).

  • Lay £10 at 2.00: liability £10.
  • Lay £10 at 5.00: liability £40.
  • Lay £10 at 21.00: liability £200.

Laying a big-priced horse or an outsider feels safe because it usually loses, but one winner can wipe out many small wins. The exchange holds your full liability from your balance until the bet is settled.

Commission

Exchanges take a percentage of your net winnings, never your stake, and nothing when you lose. Rates vary by exchange and by account. Betfair, for example, says its commission is 2%, 5% or 8% of Exchange winnings depending on the rewards package, with UK and Irish customers on the standard package paying up to 5%.

On a £10 back bet at 10.0, the £90 profit becomes £85.50 at 5% commission. So when you compare an exchange price with a bookmaker's, take the commission off first. A useful rule: at 5% commission, exchange odds of 3.00 are worth about 2.90 at a bookmaker.

Liquidity: can you actually get the price?

An exchange price is only as good as the money waiting behind it. Big football matches and major races have deep markets, often with thousands of pounds available at the top price. Small leagues, lower-grade races and early markets can be thin. The price on screen might only be there for a few pounds, and the gap between the best back and lay prices can be wide. If that gap is wide, treat the price with care: it is not a strong signal of the true chance.

Why serious bettors watch the exchange

  • Price check. The middle of a busy exchange market is one of the best guides to the real chance of an outcome. If a bookmaker's price is longer than the exchange, it may be good value. If it is shorter, it usually is not.
  • No restriction on winning. The exchange earns commission whether you win or lose, so it has less reason to limit winning customers than a bookmaker does. Some exchanges do charge extra fees to their most successful customers.
  • Trading. Because you can lay as well as back, you can take a price and lay it later at shorter odds to lock in a profit whatever happens, or cut a loss. It sounds easy. In practice most people who try it lose to commission and to faster, better-informed traders.

Things to check before you start

  • Use only an exchange licensed where you live. In Great Britain that means the Gambling Commission. Exchanges are not available everywhere: some countries do not license them at all.
  • Read how the exchange handles non-runners, abandoned matches and in-play suspensions. These rules can differ from a normal bookmaker.
  • Know your liability before you lay. Set a deposit limit, as you would with any betting account.
