Fractional to decimal odds: the full conversion table, with American odds and the chance each price implies

What does 5/2 mean? What is 5/4 as a decimal? Every standard UK price from 1/10 to 100/1 converted to decimal and American odds, the chance it implies, the two-second method to do it in your head, and how to turn a decimal price back into a fraction.

FootballHorse racingSports betting
By the BetMeisters desk28 November 20266 min read

UK bookmakers still quote many prices as fractions, such as 5/2 or 11/8. Exchanges, European sites and most apps use decimals, like 3.50 or 2.38, and US sites use American odds, such as +250. They are three ways of writing the same thing. This page converts every standard UK price, shows the chance each one implies, and explains how to do it yourself. For the wider background on odds, each-way terms and the bookmaker's margin, see our betting odds explained guide.

The quick answer: fraction to decimal

Divide the top number by the bottom number, then add 1.

  • 5/2: 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5, add 1 = 3.50.
  • 5/4: 5 ÷ 4 = 1.25, add 1 = 2.25.
  • 4/6: 4 ÷ 6 = 0.667, add 1 = 1.67.
  • Evens (1/1): 1 ÷ 1 = 1, add 1 = 2.00.

The 'add 1' is your stake coming back. A fraction shows only the profit; a decimal shows the total return, stake included. That single difference is the whole reason the two look different.

What does 5/2 mean?

5/2 (said 'five to two') means that for every £2 you stake, you win £5 profit if it wins. A £10 bet wins £25 profit, and you get £35 back in total. In decimal that is 3.50 (£10 × 3.50 = £35). The price implies a 28.6% chance: 2 ÷ (5 + 2).

What does 5/4 mean?

5/4 ('five to four') means £5 profit for every £4 staked. A £10 bet wins £12.50 profit and returns £22.50. In decimal that is 2.25, an implied chance of 44.4%: 4 ÷ (5 + 4). It is just longer than evens, so it is sometimes called 'five to four against'.

Odds-on and odds-against

When the top number is bigger, as in 5/2, the price is odds-against: you win more than you stake, and the implied chance is under 50%. When the bottom number is bigger, as in 4/6, it is odds-on: you win less than you stake, and the implied chance is over 50%. 4/6 means £4 profit for every £6 staked, so a £10 bet returns £16.67. Evens sits exactly between the two.

The chance a price implies

For a fraction, the implied chance is bottom ÷ (top + bottom). For a decimal, it is 1 ÷ decimal. So 3/1 is 1 ÷ 4 = 25%, and 1.80 is 1 ÷ 1.80 = 55.6%.

This is the price's chance, not the real chance. A bookmaker builds a margin into every market, so if you add up the implied chances of every outcome you get more than 100%. In a football match priced Home 2.10, Draw 3.40, Away 3.60, they come to 47.6% + 29.4% + 27.8% = 104.8%. The extra 4.8% is the margin. A price is only good value if the real chance is higher than the implied chance. That gap is what we measure before every BetMeisters tip.

Decimal back to a fraction

Take 1 off the decimal, then turn what is left into a fraction. 3.50 minus 1 is 2.5, which is 5/2. 2.25 minus 1 is 1.25, which is 5/4. 1.80 minus 1 is 0.8, which is 4/5.

American odds in one paragraph

A plus number is the profit on a $100 stake: +250 wins $250, the same as 5/2 or 3.50. A minus number is what you must stake to win $100: -150 means staking $150 to win $100, the same as 4/6 or 1.67. To convert: for +250, divide by 100 and add 1 (3.50). For -150, divide 100 by 150 and add 1 (1.67). Evens is +100.

Mental shortcuts

  • Anything over 1 (like 7/2): say the fraction as a number (3.5) and add 1 (4.50).
  • Odds-on (like 8/11): 8 ÷ 11 is a little over 0.7, so about 1.73.
  • Chance from a decimal: 2.00 is 50%, 3.00 is 33%, 4.00 is 25%, 5.00 is 20%, 10.00 is 10%. Most prices sit between two of these.
  • Each-way: the place part pays a fraction of the odds, usually 1/4 or 1/5. At 8/1 with 1/5 odds a place, the place part pays at 8/5 (2.60).

Why decimals are easier for comparing prices

Is 13/8 better than 8/5? In decimals it is obvious: 2.63 against 2.60. Decimals line up in a single scale, so the bigger number is always the better price, and the implied chance is one division away. That is why we quote decimals first on BetMeisters, with the fraction alongside on every tip.

Common questions

What is the difference between fractional and decimal odds? Only the stake. A fraction shows profit; a decimal shows total return including the stake. 2/1 and 3.00 are the same bet.

Is 6/4 the same as 3/2? Yes. Both are 2.50 in decimal and imply 40%. Bookmakers traditionally write 6/4.

What is 100/30? It is 10/3, or 4.33 in decimal (23.1%). The old-fashioned way of writing it survives in racing.

What does 85/40 mean? 2.125 to 1, or 3.13 in decimal (32.0%). It sits between 2/1 and 9/4.

Do the odds tell me the real chance? No. They tell you the chance the bookmaker is charging for, including its margin. Our tips compare the price with the sharpest market's chance, with the margin removed, and only go out when the price is longer.
