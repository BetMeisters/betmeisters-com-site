Over/under goals and both teams to score explained: lines, quarter goals and how to price them

Goal lines and both-teams-to-score are two of the most popular football markets. Here is how each one settles, what the odd-looking lines like 2.25 and 2.75 mean, and how to tell a fair price.

FootballSports betting
By the BetMeisters desk21 November 20267 min read

Two markets that do not care who wins are among the most bet on in football: over/under goals (how many goals in the match) and both teams to score (BTTS). Both are settled on the score after 90 minutes plus stoppage time; extra time and penalties do not count unless the market says so.

Over/under: the half-goal lines

The classic line is 2.5 goals. Over 2.5 wins with three or more goals; under 2.5 wins with two or fewer. Because the line sits on a half goal there is no draw: every bet wins or loses. Lines of 0.5, 1.5, 3.5 and 4.5 work the same way.

Whole-number lines: 2, 3

On a whole-number line such as over 3 goals, exactly three goals is a push: your stake comes back. Four or more wins, two or fewer loses. This is the same idea as a draw-no-bet market.

Quarter lines: 2.25 and 2.75

Quarter lines split your stake into two halves on the two nearest lines:

  • Over 2.25 is half on over 2 and half on over 2.5. Three or more goals: both halves win. Exactly two goals: the over-2 half is a push and the over-2.5 half loses, so you lose half your stake.
  • Over 2.75 is half on over 2.5 and half on over 3. Four or more: full win. Exactly three: the 2.5 half wins and the 3 half is a push, so you get a half win.
  • Under 2.25 and under 2.75 are the mirror images.

Quarter lines let bookmakers offer prices close to evens on games where the true number of goals sits between two whole numbers.

Both teams to score

BTTS yes wins if each team scores at least once (1-1, 2-1, 3-2 and so on). BTTS no wins if at least one team fails to score, including 0-0 and any one-sided win such as 3-0. Own goals count for the team credited with the goal.

How to tell a fair price

As with any market, turn the price into a chance (one divided by the price) and compare it with a sharp source. Take a game where a sharp bookmaker, with its margin removed, makes over 2.5 goals 55%. The fair price is 1 ÷ 0.55 = 1.82. A price of 1.90 elsewhere is about 4.5% above fair (1.90 × 0.55 = 1.045); a price of 1.75 is about 4% below.

Two things move these markets most: the attacking and defensive strength of the teams, and team news (a missing striker or goalkeeper). Weather and the importance of the game matter too, but less than many people think.

Common mistakes

  • Reading 2.25 as 'two goals is fine': on over 2.25, two goals loses half your stake.
  • Adding extra time: goals in extra time do not count for a 90-minute market.
  • Stacking goal markets in accas: each leg adds the bookmaker's margin again. See our guide to acca insurance and boosts.
