FPL for beginners: the squad, the budget, the points and the five decisions that matter most

Fantasy Premier League in plain English: how the £100m squad works, how players score, what transfers and chips are for, and the simple habits that separate a good season from a frustrating one.

FootballFantasy footballFootball
By the BetMeisters desk19 November 20268 min read

Fantasy Premier League (FPL) is the official, free fantasy game of the Premier League, and millions of people play it every season. You pick a squad of real players, they score points from what they do on the pitch each gameweek, and you make changes as the season goes on. It is not betting: there is no stake and nothing to lose but pride. This guide covers the rules you need for 2026/27 and the decisions that matter most.

The squad: 15 players, £100m, three per club

Every manager starts with a £100.0m budget to buy a 15-man squad: 2 goalkeepers, 5 defenders, 5 midfielders and 3 forwards. You can pick no more than three players from any one Premier League club. New for this season, the game can build a starting squad for you automatically if you would rather start from a template and edit it.

Each gameweek you choose a starting XI from your 15. It must have one goalkeeper, at least three defenders, at least two midfielders and at least one forward, so 3-4-3, 3-5-2, 4-3-3, 4-4-2, 4-5-1, 5-2-3, 5-3-2 and 5-4-1 are all allowed. The other four sit on your bench in an order you set.

Two things follow from the table. Defenders and goalkeepers from teams that keep clean sheets score well even without goals, and attacking midfielders are prized because a goal is worth 5 to them against 4 for a forward. Defensive contributions (clearances, blocks, interceptions and tackles, plus ball recoveries for midfielders and forwards) give steady points to busy players who never score.

Captain: the most important decision every week

Pick a captain and his points are doubled. If he does not play at all, your vice-captain's points are doubled instead. Most weeks, the captain choice moves your score more than any transfer, so give it the most thought: a premium attacker at home against a weak defence is the classic pick.

Transfers

You get one free transfer every gameweek, and unused ones roll over, up to a maximum of five saved. Every extra transfer beyond your free ones costs 4 points (a 'hit'). Beginners take too many hits: a hit only pays if the new player is likely to outscore the old one by more than four points over the weeks you keep him.

Prices move during the season as managers buy and sell. If a player you own rises in price, you only keep part of the rise when you sell him (FPL keeps half of any profit, rounded down), so do not chase price rises for their own sake.

The deadline for transfers and team changes is 90 minutes before the first kick-off of the gameweek. Miss it and your team from the previous week carries over.

Chips

In 2026/27 every manager has two sets of four chips, one set for each half of the season. The first set must be used before the Gameweek 19 deadline (13:30 GMT on Saturday 2 January):

  • Wildcard: unlimited free transfers for one gameweek, and the changes stay.
  • Free Hit: unlimited free transfers for one gameweek only; your old squad comes back afterwards.
  • Triple Captain: your captain's points are tripled instead of doubled.
  • Bench Boost: your four bench players' points count too.

Chips are most valuable in 'double gameweeks', when some teams play twice. A Bench Boost or Triple Captain in a double gameweek can be worth two normal weeks.

The five decisions that matter most

  1. Spend on the attack, save at the back. Most points come from a handful of premium attackers; cheap defenders from solid teams fill the gaps well.
  2. Fill the bench cheaply but with starters. Bench players only matter when a starter misses out, so the cheapest regular starters are enough.
  3. Captain with your head. Favour form, fixtures and home games. The betting market is a useful guide: a team's chance of scoring two or more goals says more than last week's points.
  4. Avoid knee-jerk transfers. One bad week is noise. Bank free transfers and use them when injuries or fixture swings make a real difference.
  5. Plan chips around the fixture list. Keep at least one chip for the double gameweeks in each half of the season.

Using the betting market for FPL

The odds on a match are the best free forecast available. A team priced as a strong favourite, with a low goals line against it, is a clean-sheet candidate; a team expected to score two or more is where your attackers and captain should come from.
