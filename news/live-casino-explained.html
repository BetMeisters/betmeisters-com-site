Live casino explained: how live dealer games work and what the house edge really is

Live casino streams real tables and real dealers to your screen. Here is how it works, the house edge on the main games, why game shows return less than they look, and what to check before you play.

Online casino
By the BetMeisters desk26 November 20265 min read

A live casino is a real table, a real dealer and real cards or a real wheel, filmed in a studio and streamed to your phone or computer. You place bets on screen; the dealer deals or spins; the result is read by cameras and settled automatically. It feels closer to a real casino than a computer game, but the maths of each game is exactly the same.

How it works

  • Studios: the games are run by specialist studios that supply many online casinos at once. The same table can appear under several casino brands.
  • Betting windows: you get a few seconds to place your bet before each round, so the pace is slower than a digital game.
  • Limits: each table has a minimum and maximum stake, shown before you sit down. In Great Britain the online slots stake limits do not apply to live table games, so set your own limits.

The house edge on the main games

  • Blackjack: with good rules and perfect basic strategy the house edge is around 0.5%, one of the lowest in the casino. Rules matter: a table that pays 6 to 5 on blackjack instead of 3 to 2 adds about 1.4% to the house edge, so avoid it.
  • Baccarat: about 1.06% on Banker (after the 5% commission) and 1.24% on Player. The Tie bet, paying 8 to 1, has a house edge of about 14.4%: one of the worst bets on the table.
  • Roulette: European roulette has one zero, so the house edge is 2.70% (1 in 37). American roulette adds a double zero and roughly doubles it. Choose single-zero wheels.
  • Game shows: wheel and bonus games are built for entertainment and usually return less than the classic tables. One of the best known lists a return to player of 96.08%, a house edge of about 4%, and individual bet spots can be worse. Check the game's information panel before you play.

The house edge is what the casino expects to keep from every pound staked over the long run. A 1% edge does not mean you lose 1% of your money: it means you lose about £1 for every £100 you bet, and bets are repeated many times. Our RTP guide explains return to player in more detail.

What to check before you play

  • The licence: play only with a casino licensed for your country. In Great Britain that means a Gambling Commission licence, which you can check on the public register.
  • The rules on the table: blackjack payout (3 to 2), single-zero roulette, and the commission on baccarat.
  • The game information panel: the return to player and the payouts for every bet.
  • Withdrawal terms: how long cashing out takes and when identity checks happen. Our guide to your withdrawal rights explains what UK rules say.
  • Bonus terms: many casino bonuses exclude live games or count them at a reduced rate towards wagering.

Play safely

Live games are fast to repeat and easy to chase. Set a deposit limit before you start, take breaks, and never play to win back losses. Every UK-licensed casino must offer deposit limits, time-outs and self-exclusion; our guide to deposit limits, time-outs and GAMSTOP shows how. If gambling stops being fun, free confidential help is available from the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133.
