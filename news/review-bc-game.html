Our view in one minute

BC.Game is one of the biggest crypto casinos, with its own "Originals" games such as Crash. Two things stand out, one good and one bad. The good: its terms are some of the fairest-sounding we have read, promising that restrictions are proportionate and temporary and that an investigation alone is never a reason to keep your money, and its Crash game is provably fair with a stated 1% house edge. The bad: it is licensed in Anjouan, a much lighter regime; its terms never name the company behind it; there is no independent complaints body; and in June 2026 Sweden banned its operator from offering gambling there without a licence.

Licence and where it applies

Licence: BC.Game's licence page says it is operated by Twocent Technology Limited, a company registered in Belize (number 41939), and licensed by the Government of the Autonomous Island of Anjouan, Union of Comoros, under licence ALSI-202410011-FI1. We have not been able to read the regulator's own register for this licence, so we report it as BC.Game states it, not as independently verified.

Regulatory record: on 29 June 2026 Sweden's gambling regulator, Spelinspektionen, banned Twocent Technology Limited from offering gambling in Sweden without a Swedish licence, with immediate effect, after finding Swedish-language affiliate marketing and streamer promotion aimed at Swedish players. The company did not respond to the regulator.

Where you cannot play: Australia, Austria, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Spain, the Comoros, the United Kingdom, mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau, the United States, all FATF-blacklisted countries and anywhere else the Anjouan regulator prohibits (4.3).

Crash and provable fairness

Each Crash result comes from a chain of hashes generated in advance and released in reverse order, salted with the hash of a Bitcoin block that had not been mined when the chain was published. That means the result of every round already exists before anyone bets, and you can check any round yourself with the published method. The house edge is built in at 1%: in about 1 round in 100 the multiplier is set to 1.00x. A 1% edge is low for a casino game, but it still means you lose about 1p for every pound you bet over time.

Payments and withdrawals

  • Crypto: supported, with wallet screening for sanctions and stolen funds (9.6).
  • Fees: withdrawal fees apply only where shown before you confirm (11.7); blockchain network fees are separate.
  • Checks: identity, address and source-of-funds checks can be requested before deposits or withdrawals; delays must be proportionate and last only as long as needed (11.2, 11.4).
  • Your money: the terms say customer funds remain yours, and investigations alone do not justify permanent confiscation (15.4). They do not say funds are held in a separate account.

Terms: the good and the bad

  • Good: restrictions must be objectively justified, proportionate and temporary (15.1, 15.2); genuine winnings are not reversed under the error clause (14.5).
  • Good: deposit limits, wagering limits, cooling-off periods and self-exclusion (16.3, 16.4).
  • Bad: the terms never name the company you are contracting with, and the governing law is simply "the laws specified in the Company's licensing jurisdiction" (20.4).
  • Bad: no independent dispute service; arbitration, where it applies, is under rules the company chooses (20.5).
  • Bad: VIP status can be downgraded for inactivity, and BC.Game "shall have the final determination" on VIP matters (13.8).

FAQs

Can I play at BC.Game from the UK? No. The UK is on its restricted list and an Anjouan licence does not cover UK players.

Who runs BC.Game? Its licence page names Twocent Technology Limited of Belize; its general terms do not name the company at all.

Is Crash fair? It is provably fair: every round can be checked, and the house edge is a stated 1%.
