Can an online casino hold up your withdrawal? What the £900,000 L & L Europe case says about your rights

The operator of allbritishcasino.com, casinocasino.com and hypercasino.com paid £900,000 after limiting players to one withdrawal at a time and asking for documents only when they tried to cash out. Here is what UK rules say a casino can and cannot do.

Online casinoUKLicensing and regulation
By the BetMeisters desk25 November 20264 min read

Winning is only half the story at an online casino: you also have to get your money out. A recent Gambling Commission case shows where the line is. L & L Europe Limited, which runs seven casino websites including allbritishcasino.com, casinocasino.com and hypercasino.com, agreed to pay £900,000 in a regulatory settlement published on 21 October 2026.

What went wrong with withdrawals

  • Its terms said customers could only request one withdrawal at a time, and not ask for another until the first was authorised. The Commission said this was unfair under the Consumer Rights Act 2015.
  • For some customers it manually reviewed every fifth or tenth withdrawal, which led to requests for more information before paying out. UK rules say a withdrawal must not trigger a request for information that the casino could reasonably have asked for earlier.

The investigation also found anti-money-laundering failings, and safer-gambling alerts that were switched on for some of its websites but not all of them.

Your rights in plain English

  • Checks should come early, not at cash-out. A casino can verify your identity and, in some cases, where your money comes from, but it should do that when it can reasonably ask, not use it to hold up a withdrawal.
  • Unfair terms are not enforceable. A rule that unreasonably restricts your withdrawals can breach consumer law.
  • There are legal exceptions. The Commission's Executive Director of Operations, Sue Young, said a business may sometimes need information at withdrawal to meet legal obligations, but that was not the case here.
  • If a withdrawal is stuck, complain to the casino first. If that does not resolve it within eight weeks, you can take it to the independent dispute resolution (ADR) provider named in the casino's terms.
