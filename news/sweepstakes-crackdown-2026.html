The 2026 sweepstakes casino crackdown, state by state

At least six states passed new laws against dual-currency sweepstakes casinos this year, and some reach promoters and affiliates too.

Sweepstakes and prediction marketsLicensing and regulationEnforcement
By the BetMeisters desk16 November 20265 min read
Where it's legalChecked 16 Nov 2026
Sweepstakes casinos are banned or restricted in a growing number of US states, including those listed here. Laws in several states also cover promoters and affiliates. BetMeisters.news does not link to sweepstakes casinos for readers in states where they are restricted. 18+ (21+ in some states).

Sweepstakes casinos use two currencies: one you buy for fun, and a second "sweeps" coin you can redeem for cash prizes. States have increasingly decided that this is online gambling by another name.

New laws in 2026

  • Indiana (HB 1052): bans online dual- or multi-currency sweepstakes that simulate casino or lottery games, from 1 July 2026, with civil penalties up to $100,000.
  • Iowa (SF 2289): signed 15 May 2026; sweepstakes are prohibited unless licensed or authorised, with cease-and-desist powers for the regulator.
  • Louisiana (HB 53 and HB 883): from 1 August 2026 dual-currency sweepstakes count as illegal gambling by computer, with fines up to $100,000 and up to five years in prison.
  • Maine (LD 2007): prohibits online dual-currency sweepstakes that simulate casino games, with fines of $10,000 to $100,000.
  • Oklahoma (SB 1589): passed over a veto; violations are a felony, effective 1 November 2026.
  • Tennessee (HB 1885 / SB 2136): signed 22 May 2026; operating, promoting or supporting online sweepstakes games is a consumer-protection violation.

Not just operators

A clear trend in the new laws is that liability reaches beyond the casinos to suppliers, payment firms, geolocation providers, promoters and media affiliates. That is why our sweepstakes coverage is news and explainers, and why any sweepstakes link we ever show will be checked against the reader's state first.

Washington, D.C. is weighing a different route: a bill introduced in April would legalise regulated online casino games and ban sweepstakes casinos at the same time.
